A sunflower head grows from the middle out. Each new seed appears at the center, turned a fixed angle from the one before, and pushes the older seeds outward. The whole pattern comes down to that one number: how far to turn.

Turn by a simple fraction of a circle, a half or a third, and the seeds stack into a few straight spokes with wasted space between them. Turn by almost any angle that is close to a simple fraction and you still get spokes, just curved ones. The angle that avoids every simple fraction for as long as possible is about 137.5 degrees: the golden angle.

<Phyllotaxis />

Nobody tells the plant to do this. The packing falls out of each seed growing into the biggest gap the last few left behind.
